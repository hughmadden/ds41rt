# Complete dSpark stage composition

Normalization evidence in this historical record is superseded by [the official epsilon correction](ds41-normalization-correction.md), which fixes the inherited `1e-6` RMS epsilon to `1e-20` and reruns primitive, stage and complete-draft checks.

`DsparkStage` owns one attention mHC boundary, draft attention and FFN boundary and executes them on the FFN expert stream in one CUDA graph. Attention normalization writes directly into the query-A input; attention output feeds mHC post, then ordered device copies carry residual streams and the generated pre-mix into the FFN boundary. The FFN composes normalization, router, shared/routed experts and final mHC post and publishes residual streams plus the next pre-mix only after the whole stage completes.

All request leases and committed ends are validated before metadata upload. Captured graphs retain their request count and unique cache-owner identity; replay revalidates both while holding the window borrow through synchronous GPU completion. Error paths invalidate publication and drain submitted work before child storage is released. Capture warmup is not published. This stage replaces the separately budgeted attention/mHC/FFN owners and adds no persistent allocation; the two inter-boundary device copies remain optimization candidates.

The complete stage passed on both RTX PRO 6000 GPUs for each of the three independent stages at 1, 3 and 16 requests with initial and changed-input graph replay, totaling 18 cases per GPU. The final fixture asserts bitwise BF16 equality and checks next pre-mix with absolute tolerance 1e-7. It also checks committed-ring bytes remain unchanged, budget-minus-one and invalid-stage rejection, unpublished/duplicate capture, stale positions, duplicate/released leases, foreign cache owners, unseeded slots, position overflow and recovery by a valid execution.

Synthetic attention weights and cache data reuse the independently qualified attention-wave oracle. Shared FFN weights and expert-zero FP4 weights are nonzero for all stages, while equal router scores select experts 0/1/2 with weight 0.5 each and the other experts remain zero. Shared and routed FFN contributions total 13.5 or 7.59375 in these fixtures. Initial residual factors are [1,2,3,4]; changed factors [1,-1,0,0] cancel under a newly generated equal pre but not under the incoming one-hot pre, making the shifted attention boundary observable. The FFN next pre is approximately 0.250001, distinct from attention's 0.500001, and is checked separately.

The first simplified oracle approximated uniform Sinkhorn coefficients as exactly 1/4, producing occasional one-BF16-step differences. Including the reference's epsilon in its initial column normalization and 19 subsequent row/column iterations removes those differences without changing production arithmetic. Final exact-match qualification includes those terms and preserves the official BF16 and FP8 quantization boundaries.

Device bytes per standalone stage are 20,719,172 at one request, 21,384,852 at three and 76,302,156 at sixteen; these allocations replace existing component owners, leaving the partial two-wave estimate at 8,853,577,584 bytes. Native kernels were unchanged for this composition; the final daemon and fixture builds passed against the current AOT library and master b12x pin.

This is synthetic complete-stage evidence, not real-checkpoint GPU numerical or throughput qualification. The stages are individually qualified; embedding, sequential stage-to-stage/head wiring, target verification, acceptance/rollback and production serving remain incomplete. Temporary fixture source, sparse synthetic weights and generated expected data live under `/tmp/ds41-stage/` and are not shipped source.
